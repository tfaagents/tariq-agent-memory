## FLOW 03 · Progress claim to payment schedule

The centre of the whole conversation, and the one where Heather is doing work she says is
not hers. Spans Heather, Daniel and the VA.

```
PROCESS: Progress claim to payment schedule
FREQUENCY: monthly, from the 25th   TIME IT TAKES: [to confirm]
TRIGGER: the 25th of the month passes and subcontractors email their progress claims to accounts@tfaconstructions.com.au on 14 day terms
SYSTEMS: Outlook (accounts@), Procore (via the Pro Scan email-in address), printer, Procore commitments and schedule of values, Word payment schedule template, the VA, Heather's cash flow master sheet
STEPS:
1. The progress claim arrives in the accounts inbox. Heather receives it first
2. Heather forwards it to Pro Scan, which files it onto Procore. ("Pro Scan is Procore. Same thing.")
3. Daniel picks it up from Procore, prints it, goes to site and assesses the real percentage complete against what was claimed
   - the sub fills the claim in wrong: Daniel's live example claimed 40 and 30 separately when they were actually at 80 cumulative. He assesses the correct figure regardless of what they wrote
   - the commitment has no schedule of values, only a lump sum: JM Sec at $38,000 "supply and install as per contract". "We can't actually assess it correctly through the system." Every contract signed before the new process has this problem
4. Daniel writes the assessed percentages down and sends them to the VA
5. The VA generates the payment schedule off those percentages, following their own documented process
6. The payment schedule goes out to the subcontractor. Heather is CC'd
7. Heather sees the payment schedule and moves that row into the approved section of her cash flow master sheet
8. The sub issues an invoice against the payment schedule
   - the sub disagrees with the payment schedule: TFA negotiates until they agree, then the sub invoices
   - the sub sends the invoice and the progress claim together, out of order (Wilco, Astro Clean both do this). Heather forwards it to Pro Scan anyway, which prompts Daniel to push back on them
   - the sub sends an invoice with no progress claim at all and no process behind it: "they shouldn't be sending me an invoice yet"
   - the sub sends no invoice: Heather cannot pay, because she has no bank details. "You'd be surprised how many people."
GUIDELINES: the payment schedule is the commitment, and under the BIF Act TFA has to honour it — "Heather knows that the payment schedule is the law." But Heather will not pay without an invoice, because she needs bank details, and Clay has agreed to that. Subs are still being taught the process, so a period of transition and pushback is expected rather than treated as failure. The correct order is always progress claim → assessment → payment schedule → invoice → payment; Mil Power (Marco) is the model, sent both together once, was corrected, has done it right every month since
OUTPUT: a payment schedule issued to the subcontractor with an assessed amount and a date · WHERE it lands: email to the sub, CC to Heather · WHO gets it: the subcontractor, Heather, [to confirm whether it is also filed to Procore]
CHECKS: Daniel physically walks the site and reassesses the claimed percentage rather than accepting it. Heather checks an invoice exists before paying
WAITING ON: subs who claim wrong or claim out of order; contracts with no schedule of values, which cannot be assessed in Procore at all
```
